# 5. Guarantees and assumptions

## Integrity of an accepted history

**Claim (recorded contributions and correct clearing).** Fix the batch parameters and one published final log digest. Suppose the public verifier accepts the batch for that digest and a trader checks their receipts against the same history. For each column and real tick, the final volume is at least the sum of the quantities in the trader's distinct checked transitions at that coordinate. The accepted crossing receipt gives the unique clearing tick and rationing inputs for the accumulated book. An accepted no-cross outcome establishes that ordinary demand and supply never overlap at any tick.

This is an integrity claim about one accepted history. The privacy goal is stated separately below. A receipt identifies one contribution. The update proof restricts every subsequent change to a bounded addition, so that other entries cannot cancel the checked contribution. The clearing proof uses the final commitments obtained from that same history and enforces the price rule of [Section 2](02-auction-rule.md). Together these checks tie what the trader recorded to what the exchange cleared.

The claim is conditional on a common digest, a securely generated reference string, collision resistance of the log hash, sound opening and proof checks for this construction, and consistent extraction across the shared commitments. We also require $\mu(2^{k_{\rm in}}-1)<|\mathbb{F}|$ for a log of $\mu$ steps so that accumulated quantities cannot wrap in the scalar field. [Appendix C](C-security-proofs.md) derives the contribution bound and uniqueness statement under these assumptions, including the different final-column checks at a no-cross close.

These conclusions leave the exchange control over admission. It can decline to log a submission, post additional interest, delay a close or withhold its proofs. Verification cannot tell when an order reached the exchange, who originated a contribution, or whether traders received the units due to them. The outcome is deterministic once the accepted book is fixed. That property is narrower than fairness of the market as a whole.

## Disclosure and the privacy goal

For a fixed quantity and tick, $\rho[\gamma]_1$ is uniform in the commitment group when the generator is nonzero and $\rho$ is fresh and uniform. Consequently an honest commitment difference alone reveals no information about its encoded quantity or tick. Moving all four heads on every honest submission also conceals which column carries the order in that sequence of differences. A live observer can count groups and time their publication. The exchange itself receives the plaintext, and anyone who receives a private update receipt can read the associated order fields.

The public receipt also discloses more than the final price. It reveals the plateau endpoints, maximum ordinary volume and minimum imbalance. At positive imbalance it also identifies the long side and gives $P$ and $J$. At balance these last fields are fixed to zero and disclose no actual IO depth. In a small batch a trader may infer another participant's interest from these values and their own order. For example, at positive imbalance the receipt determines aggregate better-priced long-side interest as $V_{\max}+v-P$. A trader can infer that quantity even without seeing how it is distributed across ticks. Our privacy goal allows deductions from the public output. It asks whether an observer can learn anything further from the opening, log and proofs, given the public schedule, outcome and the orders and receipt positions of any colluding traders.

We do not have a proof of joint simulation for that view. In particular, sampling uniform differences and simulating a proof about some book does not ensure that the simulated closing proof refers to exactly the commitments checked by the traders and the update verifier. The book masks are fixed at opening and reused across the recorded history. Repeating a closing proof at fresh challenge points could disclose further evaluations of those masks. Our Rust implementation derives closing randomness from private batch state so a repeat close gives the same proof bytes. That engineering measure limits one source of extra disclosure; it does not resolve the joint simulation question. Sefranek proves statistical zero knowledge for a patched specification of PLONK ([28](references.md#ref-28)). Extending that result to our protocol would require simulating commitments reused across the log and the two proofs.
